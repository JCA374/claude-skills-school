A skill that reviews papers and reports

A complete, tested skill you can copy into ~/.claude/skills today. Below: the real-life situations it's for, how it's built and why, the full files, and how to make it learn.

Where you'd use it

Real: your RL work

Before you reproduce a paper

You're about to spend a week reimplementing a new RL method for the bipedal walker. Ask first: "Review this paper. Does the gain survive the seed variance?" The ML checklist looks for best-of-N selection and untuned baselines, exactly the winner's-curse trap your own project site warns about.
Real: at work

A consultancy or vendor report

A 60-page market report lands in your inbox. The business checklist asks who paid for it, where each number comes from, and whether the summary claims more than the body shows. You get the three weakest points in five minutes.
Real: your own write-ups

Review yourself before publishing

Run it on a project's lessons page or a podcast transcript before it goes live: "do my claims follow from my numbers?" It's the same check as the podcast skill's "every number must come from a file".
Theoretical

A weekly reading list

With /schedule, a cloud agent could fetch new arXiv papers on a topic every Monday, review each with this skill, and leave you a ranked list. A cloud agent doesn't see your laptop's ~/.claude/skills, so the skill would live in the repo's .claude/skills. With voice-text, the top review becomes a 3-minute MP3 for your walk.

How it's built, and why

Design choiceLesson it applies
Description lists "review, referee, critique, sanity-check, does this hold up?" and "even if they don't say review"4: the description is the trigger
Three checklists as references/, and a table saying which one to read2: progressive disclosure
extract.py gives page-marked text and facts (n, p-values, figures)5: scripts for the exact work
check_quotes.py must exit 0 before "done"5: verification gates, like listen-check's second model
Each checklist item says why it matters8: explain the why
tests/cases.md + test papers with known flaws6: how a skill learns

Install it (2 minutes)

# from a clone of this site's repo
cp -r docs/examples/paper-review ~/.claude/skills/paper-review
# then, in any folder with a PDF:
claude "review paper.pdf, I want to know if the main result holds up"

Needs pdftotext (installed on your laptop) or the Python pypdf package (also installed).

Tested here

I ran both scripts on a two-page test PDF with a review that contained one made-up number ("91% recall") and one made-up quote ("the effect is large in every subgroup"). extract.py found 2 pages, 1 figure, 1 table, 2 references and n = 240. check_quotes.py passed the real quote and the real numbers (0.83, 0.79, 240) and flagged exactly the two made-up items, exiting with 1.

Make it learn: your first two weeks

1
Build a test set of 6 papers: 2 you know are solid, 2 with flaws you know, and 2 retracted or heavily criticised papers where the flaw is public (Retraction Watch is a good source). Write tests/papers/NN.expected.md: the flaws a good review must find.
2
Benchmark: "Use skill-creator to evaluate paper-review on tests/papers: with and without the skill." Assertion per paper: "the review names flaw X".
3
Every miss becomes a case in tests/cases.md and a rule in the right checklist, with the why. Re-run all six.
4
Tune the trigger: run skill-creator's description optimiser with near misses that should not fire ("summarise this paper", "fix the grammar in my report").
Limits to remember. A model review is a second reader, not a referee of record: it can't rerun experiments, and it can misjudge a field it knows less about. That's why the form has a confidence line, and why sources it couldn't check are marked "not verified".
